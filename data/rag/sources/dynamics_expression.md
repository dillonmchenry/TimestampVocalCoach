# Dynamics and Expression in Singing

Dynamic control — the management of volume, intensity, and timbral change over time —
is the primary carrier of emotional content in singing. Pitch defines what is sung;
dynamics and expression define how it is communicated.

## Dynamic Shaping at Different Scales

**Note-level dynamics**: Crescendo, decrescendo, swell (crescendo-decrescendo), and sforzando
(sharp accent) within a single note. These are fine-grained tools of expression that shape
individual words and syllables.

**Phrase-level dynamics**: Volume changes across a multi-note phrase. A phrase may build
toward its final note, or decay from a peak at the beginning. Phrase-level dynamics contribute
to the sense of direction and arrival in a musical line.

**Section-level dynamics**: The contrast between sections (quiet verse, loud chorus) that
creates the structural architecture of the song. These broad contrasts are among the most
immediately perceptible features of a performance and are central to the emotional impact of
pop song structure.

## The Messa di Voce

The messa di voce — sustaining a single note from pianissimo through fortissimo and back to
pianissimo — is the classical exercise for developing independent control of volume and pitch.
The challenge is that increasing subglottic pressure (for crescendo) tends to sharpen pitch,
requiring compensating relaxation of the cricothyroid. Singers who can execute a clean messa
di voce have developed the coordination to decouple dynamics from pitch.

This exercise is considered foundational in classical and bel canto training because it
simultaneously develops breath control, phonatory coordination, and registration management.

## Breath and Dynamics

Volume is primarily a function of subglottic pressure: more pressure produces louder tone.
However, the relationship is not linear — above certain thresholds, increased pressure causes
the vocal folds to "blow apart" and the tone becomes pressed rather than louder. Optimal
loudness involves increasing pressure while maintaining efficient adduction.

Decreasing volume (pianissimo) requires reducing pressure while maintaining enough fold
tension to sustain phonation. This is technically demanding and requires more precise
laryngeal control than forte singing.

## Common Dynamic Problems

**Dynamic flatness**: Singing at uniform volume across sections that should contrast.
This eliminates the structural architecture of the song and reduces its emotional impact.

**Support fade**: Volume decreasing within a sustained note due to breath depletion rather
than intentional decrescendo. Distinguishable by its involuntary quality and association
with pitch flatting.

**Abrupt cutoffs**: Ending notes by closing suddenly rather than tapering, which disrupts
the musical flow and can sound percussive rather than vocal.

**Over-pushsing**: Forcing volume above what efficient technique provides, causing pressed
tone and pitch sharpness, often associated with registration strain above the passaggio.

## Expression Techniques

**Timbral dynamics**: Changing tone colour (breathiness, fullness, brightness) as a parallel
expressive channel to volume dynamics. A soft note can still have a full, rich tone; a loud
note can still have a forward, bright quality rather than a pressed one.

**Legato and phrase shaping**: Volume contours that follow the natural linguistic and melodic
stress of the phrase create a perception of flowing, connected singing. Choppy dynamics that
don't align with text stress disrupt the legato line.

**Stylistic conventions**: Different genres have different dynamic expectations. Classical
singing prizes wide dynamic range with precise control. Contemporary pop often uses compression
to reduce dynamic range, so a singer's natural dynamic variation may need to adjust for style.
